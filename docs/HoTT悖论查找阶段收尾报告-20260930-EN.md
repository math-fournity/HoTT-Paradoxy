## 0. In one sentence

Whether two things are the same ought to be a simple question. To make HoTT economical, it treats things with the same structure as the same (univalence); to let one theory accommodate more geometry, it includes circles, spheres and shapes of every dimension (higher inductive types). Together, these choices make “are they the same?” lead to another question at each level, with no final finite level in the cases described here. The researcher reads this as a reality-relative paradox with Zeno's shape and judges that we have very likely found one.

## 1. What we are looking for

- From the beginning, the researcher said the target was not an internal contradiction in HoTT but the theory's non-reality. The most elegant result would be an “impossible difficulty” that appears after we “think in HoTT,” even though it does not occur in reality (【Original words】KC-000010).
- Two kinds: something that can be completed in reality but not in the theory; and something that cannot be completed in reality, yet the theory bypasses ASK—first asking whether the question is well-founded and finishable—and treats it as complete (【Original words】KC-000022).
- Method: a theory may simplify or idealize a condition to be economical or to make one method work in more settings. The research asks whether a task that depends on the altered condition can make the consequence visible. This is more targeted than listing examples without a guiding premise (【Original words】KC-000047, KC-000048).
- On 2026-09-30, the researcher gave an operational definition (【Original words】KC-000054):

> `UR` = “Something that should be very simple cannot be done, even in theory X”; the researcher said this is an “unreasonableness” resembling Zeno's paradox.

  “Reality” means the first half of UR—something that should be very simple. “Paradox” means the complete UR statement. The judge is a person who sees it and recognizes the problem at a glance.
- The same day, the researcher reiterated that the project is looking for HoTT's reality-relative paradoxes, without adding “existence” as a qualifier that narrows the target (【Original words】KC-000053).

## 2. What we found

**The thing that should be simple:** deciding whether two things are the same.

**The process designed to test it:** a program asks, level by level, “Has the sameness between these things been settled by level k? If not, ask at a higher level.” At every level, a rule with a proof answers yes or no. Mathematically, this corresponds to checking whether an object belongs to a particular h-level. For now, think of an h-level as a standard way to describe how many layers of sameness a type has; exact definitions are in `CLAIMS.md` and the corresponding proof files. A yes answer stops the program and reports the level. This is a precise model used to test the reading; it is not a complete formalization of the researcher's original ring paradox.

## 3. Why say “we have very likely found it”?

- **The shape fits:** This is the first kind of paradox. In the control world where sameness is a fact, the question is settled at once; in HoTT it never is (KC-000022). Its “cannot be completed” has Zeno's shape: every finite stage is definitely short of arrival, and the specified program does not stop. It is not the other kind—“every level can be built, but no uniform method is known”—which is distinguished in exposition shard 003.
- **The target is specific:** Each control changes one suspected premise at a time:
  - Treat sameness as a fact: question 1 stops.
  - Cap the height: it stops at the cap.
  - Use truncation: question 1 stops, but the object has changed (KC-000048).
- **The reasoning survives checking:** “Never stops” is an internal theorem of HoTT, not a report that a computer ran for a long time. C-77 through C-80 replayed consistently on Linux and macOS.
- **Who judges:** Under UR, “unreasonable” is judged by a person seeing the situation. After reading the evidence, the researcher said 【Original words】: “其实看了你捕捉到的HoTT的内容，我已经闻到了这种`不合理`的味道。”

  **Translation:** “After seeing what you have captured about HoTT, I can already sense this ‘unreasonableness.’”

## 4. Attribution: which condition was changed?

- Candidate premises:
  - Univalence: isomorphic things count as the same; the benefit is economy.
  - Higher inductive types: shapes of arbitrary dimension are available at once; the benefit is generality.
  - Handing the theory-user in one step an operation to perform on every possible input (mathematicians use a Π-type for a function covering all inputs).
  - The universe is the object being questioned.
  - The correspondence: does our level-by-level question really ask the simple question the researcher named—“Are these two things the same?”
  - The reality-side premise: sameness is a fact that can be checked once.
- Distinguishing evidence:
  - Replace structural sameness with sameness as a fact, and the process stops.
  - Cap the height, and it stops.
  - Replace the universe with an ordinary product, and it still never stops; therefore the universe is not necessary.
  - A height-bounded product is also handed over all at once, yet the program stops; therefore “all inputs are supplied at once” does not by itself explain the endless questioning.
- 【AI's interpretive judgment】(conditional on the researcher's “simple task”): first examine the rule that lets isomorphic things count as the same (univalence), then the way a single theory makes shapes of arbitrary dimension available (higher inductive types). High-dimensional shapes are not unique to HoTT: each homotopy group of the product used here is ℤ. That is background reported by mathematical sources; this project has not separately machine-proved it. The current judgment is that the difference worth examining first may be what “sameness” means. Both choices contribute to the result, so this is an order for investigation, not a claim that one unique cause has been identified.
- The strongest alternative reading is “you changed the question”: asking about set truncation stops at question 1. The response is that truncation answers “how many branches?”; the object has changed, and one cannot decode back (CG001-C-83). This is the same kind of issue as “does keeping a separate ledger count as completion?” The researcher used UR to answer what the reality-side task is: the thing that should be simple.
- Keep the two directions separate. This report concerns “possible in reality, but the theory keeps asking forever.” The other direction is “not completed in reality, yet the theory treats it as delivered.” The latter can be compared with the user's Russell-style analysis, but this report does not prove that the two are the same mechanism (KC-000050–000052).

## 5. What this is not

- It is not an internal contradiction in HoTT. The proof checker accepts all the reasoning inside HoTT.
- Most of the mathematical facts are not new. The product without a finite level is Example 8.8.6 of the HoTT Book. For the universe itself, the book says it is expected to be possible to prove that it is not an n-type for any n, but that this had not yet been done (§8.8, final paragraph). Kraus–Sattler (2015) proved that the nth universe in the univalent hierarchy is not an n-type, without higher inductive types. Informally, an n-type is a technical classification saying that its sameness structure stops at level n. This repository machine-proves that a single universe with higher inductive types is not an n-type for any n (C-75); whether later literature published such a proof remains to be checked. What is new is mainly the reading and the premise it points toward; that reading has not yet been checked against the literature. (Source correction, 2026-09-30: the first draft said experts already knew both the universe and product had no finite level; that overstated what was known about the universe.)
- It is not “in every case.” The result concerns the universe and objects whose heights are unbounded. But the universe is not an edge case: it is the object in univalence, and a member of the domain HoTT cannot refuse to discuss (KC-000050).
- “Never stops” is first a proof inside the theory. To read it as “a real computer running this program will never finish,” one must also assume the theory is consistent and that reasoning about closed computations in the theory corresponds to the expected execution behavior (canonicity). This report proves neither extra condition. The existence of a cubical-set model for cubical type theory is a fact reported by the source material.

## 6. Other lines: not conclusions of this report

“Infinite coherence” is another Zeno-like candidate proposed by the project AIs; it is not what the researcher meant by “the ghost of Zeno's paradox” on 2026-09-27. Its finite-level examples have been written down and checked. Whether one definition can cover all levels at once remains an open question; it has not been proved impossible.

Other topics—motion measurement, whether states are the same, and counting steps on a round trip—have their own questions, some of whose specific judgments still await the researcher. This report does not reassess them. Whether the original ring paradox can be represented faithfully in current HoTT also remains open. A reliable correspondence between one restoration model and Markov's principle (a logical principle about search and existence) is likewise conjectural. A broader survey of HoTT's theoretical choices is separate work; this report does not claim to have surveyed the whole theory.

## 10. What this report does not claim is complete

- It does not say that the ordinary question “Are these two things the same?” has been answered. It reports that asking it level by level has no finite endpoint on the specified HoTT objects.
- It does not prove the researcher's entire philosophical interpretation, or that the original ring paradox has been formalized faithfully in HoTT.
- Univalence is the current first premise to examine, not a uniquely established cause; the proof results do not declare an internal contradiction in HoTT.
