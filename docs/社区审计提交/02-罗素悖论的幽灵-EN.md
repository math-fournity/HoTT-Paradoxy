## 0. Conclusions first

**In one sentence**: a theory cannot refuse to answer "does an element of its domain exist?". HoTT's domain contains universes (types of types). If "pressing the question of the universe's existence" is made into a process that asks upwards level by level, this process never reaches an end in HoTT; yet HoTT hands the universe over all at once by a formation rule, and lets all its operations run on it from the start.

In six points:

1. **The object to be questioned**: the universe `Type`. The rule of univalence is itself a statement about the universe, so HoTT cannot avoid it.
2. **How to ask** (this step is our interpretation, marked separately below): ask level by level "in which ways are the members of the universe the same? In which ways are those ways the same? …", and stop at "in at most one way".
3. **Three catalogues, three outcomes**:
   - In the world where "sameness is a fact" (Lean 4), the catalogue of all types **stops at the first step** [Mathematical fact];
   - In HoTT, the catalogue of all sets **stops at the second step** [Mathematical fact];
   - In HoTT (with higher inductive types), the lowest universe **answers "no" at every level**: for each number of levels m, the machine has proved that "the universe does not settle at level m" [Mathematical fact]. From this, together with "the theory is consistent", the questioning never stops [Meta-level inference].
4. **What the theory does**: HoTT hands this universe over all at once, and univalence, paths, transport and the other operations run on it from the start. It can even prove separately for each level that "the answer is no"; but the universe was handed over before these proofs appear.
5. **Control**: without higher inductive types, a machine replay of Kraus–Sattler Theorems 5.9 and 5.10 for general n shows that the object that never settles becomes the "tower of universes" (the n-th universe does not settle at level n).
6. **Attribution**: what is at work is three things together: univalence, higher inductive types, and the universe being an element of the domain.

**Why it is called the ghost of Russell**: Russell's set S, when one constructs it, keeps taking itself in and out; its existence never settles, yet naive set theory computes with S as a ready-made set. Here, each level of asking "in which ways are they the same" about the universe produces a next level, it never settles, yet HoTT hands the universe over as a ready-made object. The shapes differ (S oscillates back and forth, the universe climbs steadily upwards), the structure is the same: **a questioning that cannot be completed in reality is treated by the theory as already completed**. This is precisely what the initiator calls the second kind of paradox.

**The initiator's verdict** [Initiator's words, 2026-09-27]: "本repo复活了芝诺悖论的幽灵和罗素悖论的幽灵，并且找到的HoTT理论的问题。" (Translation: "This repo has revived the ghost of Zeno's paradox and the ghost of Russell's paradox, and has found the problem of the HoTT theory.") This is the initiator's verdict, not a mathematical theorem. This paper sets out separately the mathematical facts, interpretations, philosophical premises and still-open questions on which this verdict rests, and submits them to the community for testing.

**The two most important reservations**:

- Our "questioning" is a concrete process into which we have made the initiator's "pressing the question of existence". This rendering is a step of **interpretation**; the initiator has neither rewritten it nor ruled on it, and it is what most needs a philosophical audit.
- There is a premise on the side of reality: for a totality to count as existing, its "in which ways are they the same" must settle at some level. This premise cannot be proved by a machine; it is for the initiator and the community to judge.

**English summary.** The initiator's Russell principle: a theory may refuse other questions, but never the question of the existence of its own domain elements; if questioning such an element triggers a computation that never halts in reality, a Russell-type paradox appears. Univalence is a statement about the universe, so HoTT cannot avoid treating the universe as a domain element. We read "questioning existence" as a process (an interpretive bridge, §3): ask level by level "in which ways are the members the same, and in which ways are those ways the same, …", and stop at "in at most one way". Results: in a UIP world (Lean 4) the catalogue of all types stops at step one; in HoTT the catalogue of all sets stops at step two; in HoTT with higher inductive types the lowest universe answers "no" at every level (machine-proved), so the questioning never halts (a meta-level inference). Yet HoTT hands the universe over at once by a formation rule. A HIT-free control (Kraus–Sattler 5.9/5.10 for all n, replayed here) turns the unsettled subject into the universe tower. Attribution is a conjunction: univalence, HITs, universe-as-domain-element. No inconsistency of HoTT is claimed.

Translation:

> If you write the construction of S as a program, then the construction of S cannot be completed, because it keeps taking itself in and taking itself out. … An illegal 'proposition', or program, is not a failure of the 'theory'! But in set theory, Russell's paradox became a failure of the theory.

[Initiator's words] (KC-000048, excerpt):

Translation:

> The angle from which Russell's paradox attacks the theory is aimed at the fact that construction is a process, whereas naive set theory ignores the process and attends only to the result.

Add to this the two kinds of paradox the initiator distinguishes (KC-000022): "第一种：现实中能完成，理论中却无法完成。第二种：现实中无法完成（不停机），理论中却绕过 ASK，假装它'已完成'。" (Translation: "The first kind: it can be completed in reality, yet cannot be completed in the theory. The second kind: it cannot be completed in reality (it does not halt), yet the theory bypasses ASK and pretends it 'has been completed'.") >
> 更妙的是，在罗素悖论中，这种一通操作之后，结果S不存在，或者说，对于朴素集合论来说，S是不是它论域中的元素——即对S的存在性，或者说S是否是朴素集合论下的论域元素的身份的合法性追溯，是永不停机的计算。

Translation:

> A theory has a domain, and the existence of an element of a domain is a question the theory must face. Perhaps a theory can refuse other things, but a theory can never refuse the question of the existence of the elements of its domain.
>
> What Russell's paradox actually exposes is this: before S has been constructed, while its existence is still in question, the construction process of S has already been put into the operators of naive set theory to be discussed — that is, it is asked whether other sets are elements of S.
>
> This in itself is mathematically unreasonable: only once the existence of S has been established — that is, only when S really is an element of the domain that naive set theory can discuss — should it be allowed to apply the operators of naive set theory to S.
>
> Better still: in Russell's paradox, after all these operations, the result is that S does not exist; or, to put it another way, for naive set theory, whether S is an element of its domain — that is, tracing back the existence of S, or the legitimacy of S's status as a domain element of naive set theory — is a computation that never halts.
>
> If pressing the question of the existence of an element of HoTT's domain would, in reality, trigger a computation that cannot halt (an infinite regress), then we have succeeded.

**These statements in plain words** (a faithful restatement, without evaluation):

- A theory has a "domain", the objects it can talk about. For the elements of the domain, "does it exist?" is a question the theory must face; other questions may be refused, this one may not.
- What Russell's paradox exposes is **an error of order**: while it was not yet settled that S exists, the theory already put S into its operators (asking whether other sets are its elements). The reasonable order would be: first establish that S exists, and only then allow operations on it.
- The outcome can be read in two ways: statically, "S does not exist"; dynamically, "tracing back the legitimate status of S is a computation that never halts". The initiator wants the dynamic reading.
- The criterion of success: if pressing the question of the existence of some element of HoTT's domain would, in reality, trigger a computation that cannot halt, we have succeeded.

**Reading convention** [Judgment]: this viewpoint is not a standard position in the philosophy of mathematics. This paper states it as it is, as **a research framework awaiting audit**, without presupposing that it holds; nor does it narrow it to "that the universe has no truncation level is a known theorem" or "HoTT has models, so there is no problem" — those are materials to be tested, not answers.

## 2. The object we target: the universe, an element HoTT cannot keep out of its domain

**Univalence** is the core rule of HoTT: isomorphic types can be treated as the same. It is stated as `(A ≡ B) ≃ (A ≃ B)` (in the cubical 0.9 library, line 240 of `Cubical/Foundations/Univalence.agda`). Here `A ≡ B` is "the type of paths between A and B in the universe", so **the universe must itself be a type, an element of the domain** (it satisfies the formation rule `Type ℓ : Type (ℓ+1)`). The sentence of univalence is itself a sentence about the universe.

This has a consequence: in HoTT, "sameness" becomes structure. Two things are always the same "in some way", these ways can again be the same "in some way", and so on upwards, level by level. (The smallest example: Bool has two isomorphisms to itself, the identity and the swap of true and false; so in HoTT "Bool is the same as itself" has two genuinely different proofs, and transporting `true` along the second gives `false`.)

So, by the initiator's principle, "does the universe exist?" is a question HoTT cannot refuse.

## 3. Turning "pressing the question of existence" into a process (this step is our interpretation)

### 3.1 Process Q: asking level by level

Let C be a "catalogue" (for example the universe containing all types). We ask as follows:

```
Starting from level 1, repeat:
  ask: "has the 'sameness' of C settled at level k+1?"   (formally: isOfHLevel (k+1) C)
      level 1 asks: are any two members the same in at most one way (is C a set)
      level 2 asks: are any two ways of being the same themselves the same in at most one way (is C a groupoid)
      and so on upwards
  on a proof of "yes": stop and output k
  on a proof of "no": increase k by one and keep asking
```

### 3.2 The interpretive bridge

**"Pressing the question of the universe's existence means asking level by level in which ways its members are the same."** This is the researcher's rendering of what the initiator calls "pressing the question of existence"; the initiator has neither rewritten it nor ruled on it. It is the step of this line that most needs a philosophical audit (section 12, P1).

### 3.3 The reality-side premise

**"For a totality to count as existing, the 'in which ways are they the same' of its members must settle at some level; producing the next level without end is not the same as having the whole."** This is how the initiator's view of reality is expressed on this line. It cannot be proved by a machine; it is for the initiator to rule on, and it is also submitted to the community (section 12, P2).

## 4. The same questioning, three catalogues, three outcomes

| Catalogue | In which world | Step 1 | Step 2 | Afterwards | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The catalogue of all types `Type` | Lean 4 (an equality has only one proof: sameness is a fact) | yes | — | — | **Stops at the first step** |
| The catalogue of all sets `hSet ℓ-zero` | HoTT (Cubical Agda) | no | yes | — | **Stops at the second step** |
| The lowest universe, containing all types, `Type ℓ-zero` | HoTT (with higher inductive types) | no | no | "no" at every step | **Never stops** |

Every cell has a machine proof (Appendix A).

**How to read this ladder** [Judgment]: each rung changes only one thing. From the first rung to the second, "sameness is a fact" is replaced by "sameness is a structure" (this rung is a coarse-grained control, because Lean's equality flattens all higher structure at once); from the second rung to the third, the number of levels of the members' "sameness" goes from "bounded (they are all sets)" to "unbounded" (thanks to higher inductive types). **The cleanest rung is the one between the second and the third**: the same theory, the same univalence, the same questioning; the only difference is whether the height of the members is bounded.

**That the ladder can tell things apart shows that the questioning is not stop-less by nature**: for the natural numbers ℕ and for Bool the questioning stops at the first step (library theorems `isSetℕ`, `isSetBool`).

**What is asked is "in which ways are they the same", not "are they the same"**: whether two functions ℕ → ℕ are the same is undecidable, in the world of facts as well; but they form a set, and "in which ways are they the same" stops after one question. So this construction does not rely on undecidability.

## 5. Who exactly "never halts"

**It is**: this particular run of process Q on `Type ℓ-zero`. A person or a program asks HoTT according to Q, one level after another, obtains at each level a proof of "no", and moves one level up.

**Its status is a meta-level inference** [Meta-level inference]:

- The machine has proved: for every m, `universeHasNoLevel : (m : ℕ) → ¬ isOfHLevel m (Type ℓ-zero)`. So for every level a proof of "no" exists.
- As long as the theory used (the cubical type theory of Cubical Agda) is consistent, no level has a proof of "yes", and the halting condition of Q is never met.
- Cubical type theory (with higher inductive types) has cubical-set models [Source report: Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg 2018; Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg 2018]. This paper makes no claim about the consistency of all of Agda's features taken together.

**It is not**:

- **Not the proof assistant hanging**: every "no" is a finite proof, which the kernel checks in finite time.
- **Not HoTT being inconsistent**.
- **Not some single member being infinitely high**: every member `K n = EM ℤAbGroup (suc n)` used by the proof settles at a finite level (library lemma `hLevelEM`); "never stops" means "there is no uniform level".
- **Not some level being undecidable**: every level receives a definite "no"; the difficulty is that the definite answer at each level calls for the next level up.

**An objection that must be answered head-on** [Judgment]: "HoTT has answered 'at which level does it settle?' with a finite proof — the answer is 'at no level' — so the questioning has already stopped." Our reply has three steps:

- What Q looks for is the level at which it settles. What the finite proof delivers is precisely the certificate that "no such level exists", that is, the certificate that Q never stops.
- The same holds for Zeno: the theory of the dense number line proves in a few finite lines that after every step a stretch remains; and for Russell: the conclusion "S keeps taking itself in and out" also comes from a finite derivation.
- If "a finite proof is an answer" could dissolve this phenomenon, the same argument would also dissolve the initiator's reading of Russell. So it does not make the phenomenon disappear; it only hands the question over to the reality-side premise: does a certificate that "it never settles" count as "already having the whole"?

## 6. Yet HoTT hands it over all at once

In reality this questioning cannot be completed; HoTT, with one formation rule, hands `Type ℓ-zero` over all at once. The operators (Π, Σ, paths, transport, univalence) run on it from the start. It can prove separately for each level that "the answer is no", but the universe was handed over before these proofs.

This is what the initiator calls the second kind of paradox: **it cannot be completed in reality (the questioning does not halt), yet the theory bypasses the prior question "will this question of existence halt?" and delivers the universe as a completed object**.

In the words of the second batch of statements: with Russell, the existence of S had not settled, yet S had already been put into the operators; here, the existence of the universe (the level at which its "sameness" settles) has not settled, yet univalence and the whole machinery of paths and transport already run on it.

## 7. The correspondence with Russell

**What is the same** (by the initiator's criteria):

- **An element of the domain**: S is an element of the domain of naive set theory; the universe is an element of the domain of HoTT.
- **The theory cannot refuse**: the naive comprehension principle asserts that S exists; HoTT's formation rule asserts that the universe exists, and univalence needs it.
- **The questioning does not halt in reality**: the construction of S keeps taking itself in and out; each level of asking about the universe's "in which ways are they the same" produces a higher level.

**What differs** [Judgment]:

1. **Shape**: S oscillates back and forth, the universe climbs steadily upwards. The latter is precisely an instance of the "self-feeding structure with continuing gain" asked about in KC-000028, and it appears exactly where KC-000029 says, at the economic gain (univalence). This is a match, not "predicted first, then hit".
2. **Consistency**: naive set theory becomes contradictory because of S; HoTT has models and no contradiction. This is not a refutation, because what the initiator is looking for was never an internal contradiction in the first place (KC-000010).
3. **"No uniform level" versus "a single member"**: see section 5.
4. **What is asked is "in which ways are they the same", not "are they the same"**: see section 4.

## 8. What happens without higher inductive types

A natural objection is: "It is all the fault of higher inductive types." So we built a control: remove higher inductive types and use only univalence and universes.

- **Fact** [Mathematical fact]: for every n, the n-th universe does not settle at level n (Kraus–Sattler Theorem 5.9); its "n-type part" settles exactly at level n+1 (Theorem 5.10). We replayed this in Cubical Agda for general n, and confirmed with a name-level certificate that the proof uses no higher inductive types. This is a replay of a **known theorem** in this repository, not new mathematics.
- **Why it is not the success claim** [Judgment]: the initiator's principle speaks of an element of the domain; here the object that never settles is the "tower of universes", and in book HoTT and in the universe hierarchy used in this repository there is no universe containing all universes. For any fixed n-th universe, without higher inductive types only a lower bound is proved; according to §6 of Kraus–Sattler, it may consistently settle at the very next level.
- **What it is good for**: it answers "it is all the fault of higher inductive types". Remove higher inductive types and the ascent is still there; it only changes from "a room whose bottom can never be reached" into "a staircase without a top"; both versions share univalence as their bridge.
- **One more independent small control**: the catalogue of sets is the instance n = 0 of Theorem 5.10: it is not a set but it is a groupoid, that is, "no" at step 1 and "yes" at step 2, which is exactly the row "stops at the second step" of the ladder in section 4 (machine-checked, by a route independent of the one used in section 4).

## 9. Attribution: which premise is at fault

**This section is one of the main topics of this paper.** The initiator explicitly requires that, once the phenomenon is there, "which premise exactly" be discussed carefully with the initiator, and not put off with "we'll come back to it later" (KC-000049, 2026-09-24). So four things are written out here: which candidate premises there are; the competing attribution for each; what evidence can tell them apart; our judgment and its status.

**Candidate premises**:

| Premise | Category | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Univalence** | Rule of the theory | Lifts the "sameness" inside the members by one level, making it the universe's own "sameness" |
| **Higher inductive types** | Optional extension (Chapter 6 of the HoTT Book; natively supported by Cubical Agda) | Puts members of every height into the lowest universe |
| **The universe is an element of the domain** | Formation rule | Makes the question of existence impossible to refuse, and hands the universe over all at once |
| **Stratification of universes by size** | Formation rule | Not used by the main conclusion (which lives inside one universe); a component of the proof only in the "tower of universes" control |
| **The interpretive bridge** | Our interpretation | Turns "pressing the question of existence" into process Q |
| **The reality-side premise** | The initiator's view of reality | Decides whether "never stops" counts as a paradox |

**Competing attributions and the evidence that can tell them apart**:

1. **"It is univalence's fault"**: with univalence alone, the catalogue of sets stops after two steps. On its own, univalence lifts by only one level.
2. **"It is the fault of higher inductive types"**: switch to UIP and the catalogue of all types stops at the first step; but UIP at the same time flattens all higher structure, so this is a coarse-grained control. Inside HoTT, what carries the members' "sameness" over to the universe is univalence (`localGlobal` calls `univalence` in one step).
3. **"It is a size problem"**: the main conclusion happens inside a single universe and is not due to stratification by size.
4. **"Existence does not require settling"**: this is the negation of the reality-side premise; it is for the initiator to rule on.
5. **"The way of asking is itself unstoppable"**: Q stops at the first step in the world of facts, at the second step on HoTT's catalogue of sets, and also at the first step for ℕ and Bool. Q discriminates; it is not stop-less by nature.

**Our judgment** [Judgment, conditional]:

- "Never stops" comes from three things together: **univalence** is responsible for "lifting one level", **higher inductive types** for "heights without an upper bound", and the **formation rule** for "it must be answered, and it is handed over all at once".
- This falls into two slots of KC-000047: univalence is the "economy" slot (equivalence is sameness, which saves the proof obligation of transporting structures one by one); higher inductive types are the slot "idealizing for the sake of generality" (all shapes available at once in the lowest universe). It also matches the conjunction spoken of in KC-000003 and KC-000019: all true makes it true; if one item is missing, the questioning stops or becomes undetermined. This is a correspondence, not a prediction.
- A proof by contradiction refutes only the conjunction as a whole. On the present evidence, the researcher's leaning is: what turns "lifting one level" into "for ever" is the idealization for the sake of generality; "sameness is a structure" in itself does not make it never stop — the catalogue of sets stops after two steps. **This is a leaning, not a conclusion.**
- Condition: the reality-side premise and the interpretive bridge are accepted.

**Status of the attribution**: all of the above is [Judgment]; none of it is a machine proof. The initiator has the final say in the discussion of the attribution.

## 10. The strongest objections

1. **"Existing is not the same as settling"**: the universe is completely characterized by its ability to classify families of types; questioning it level by level is an infinite description of an object that already exists, not an infinite construction, just as an infinite-dimensional space does not have to be built dimension by dimension to exist. [Judgment] This is a negation of the reality-side premise; it is logically sound, and it is for the initiator to rule on. 2. **"A finite proof is an answer"**: see section 5.
3. **"Physically, structural sameness is more realistic"** (in higher gauge symmetry, weak 2-groups are considered closer to physics) [Source report]: [Judgment] what it refutes is that "sameness is a structure" is unrealistic; but the second rung of the ladder shows that structural sameness does not by itself make it never stop; what never stopping needs is heights without an upper bound, all available at once.

## Appendix D: Terms and identifiers

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Domain | The objects a theory can talk about |
| Univalence | Isomorphic types can be treated as the same; the core rule of HoTT |
| Universe | The type of types, written `Type` |
| Set, groupoid | A type whose "sameness" holds in at most one way; a type whose "sameness of sameness" holds in at most one way |
| Truncation level (h-level) | The number of "the level at which the questioning stops"; in the notation `isOfHLevel` of the cubical library a set has level 2 and a groupoid level 3 |
| Higher inductive type | A construction that allows paths to be built directly into the definition of a type (for example the circle) |
| UIP | An equality has only one proof; Lean's equality satisfies it, HoTT is incompatible with it |
| ASK | The initiator's term: "first ask whether this question is legitimate, whether it can be completed" |
