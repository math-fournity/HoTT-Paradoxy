## Standard answers and their proper scope

1. **Completed-totality practice response.** Feferman reports that ordinary mathematical practice treats some totalities as definite. This is a source-reported philosophical/mathematical-practice stance; it does not prove a physical process is complete.
2. **Predicative restriction.** Predicativist programs restrict formation principles. This is a family of alternative foundations, not a proof that ZFC’s current consumer contracts are all invalid.
3. **Explicit computational constructions.** Koepke–Koerwien show what a restricted truth/computation device supplies. This controls a leap from sethood to computation; it does not settle every foundational reading.
4. **Reality bridge.** Dochtermann gives a finite-to-totality metaphor. Its qualifier blocks an automatic same-Done inference.

The run’s result is a calibration: a later claim that the user-P has no community antecedent, or that historical impredicativity settles it, must be rejected unless it distinguishes the listed fields.
